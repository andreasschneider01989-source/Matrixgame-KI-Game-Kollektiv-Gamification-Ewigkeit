# Social Connection, Violence and Relationship Trends — Germany and Global Context

**Research snapshot:** 2026-11-25  
**Status:** Evidence-aware synthesis; descriptive statistics, not causal proof  
**Primary scope:** Germany, reporting year 2025; selected global prevalence context  
**Intended use:** Matrixgame / Collective AI learning, social safety and responsible gamification

## 1. Executive summary

The current evidence does **not** support one simple story such as “violence is rising everywhere” or “people no longer want relationships.”

For Germany in 2025, two patterns coexist:

- The overall police-recorded crime total fell by 5.6% and recorded violent crime fell by 2.3% compared with 2024.
- At the same time, the latest federal situation reports show increases in recorded female victims across the monitored gender-specific offence areas and a new high in police-recorded domestic violence.

Relationship statistics are also mixed:

- Divorces rose slightly in 2025 compared with 2024, but remained 39.2% below the 2003 high.
- Marriages reached their lowest annual count since the start of the series in 1950.
- More people live alone and household forms have diversified, but this does not directly measure whether people want intimacy, sex, friendship or long-term partnership.

The most defensible interpretation is **a differentiated social picture**: serious ongoing violence and protection gaps coexist with continuing interest in relationships, while living arrangements and expectations change.

## 2. Violence in Germany — what the 2025 figures show

### 2.1 General crime and violent crime

The Federal Government's summary of the 2025 Police Crime Statistics, published 20 April 2026, reports:

- Approximately 5.5 million recorded offences overall, down 5.6% from 2024.
- Recorded violent crime down 2.3% from 2024.
- Recorded rape cases up 9.0% from 2024; the recorded count was about 72% higher than in 2018.

These are police-recorded data. They describe offences known to and recorded by police, not every violent experience in the population.

Source: [German Federal Government — Police Crime Statistics 2025, 20 April 2026](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/polizeiliche-kriminalstatistik-2025-2422020).

### 2.2 Domestic and gender-specific violence

On 24 November 2026, the Federal Ministry for Education, Family Affairs, Senior Citizens, Women and Youth reported the BKA's 2025 situation reports:

- 280,129 police-recorded victims of domestic violence, up 2.8% from 2024.
- 179,940 victims of partner violence, up 2.3%.
- 100,189 victims of intra-family violence, up 3.7%.
- 70.6% of recorded domestic-violence victims were female; for partner violence, 79.4% were female.
- More than 142,800 women and girls were recorded as victims of partner violence.
- 54,675 women and girls were recorded as victims of a sexual offence, up 2.3%.
- 31,221 female victims of digital violence were recorded, up almost 16%; common recorded forms included coercion, threats and stalking.
- 819 misogynistic offences within politically motivated crime were recorded, almost 50% more than in 2024. The ministry notes that many were insults, propaganda offences and incitement; 54 were recorded as misogynistic violent offences.
- 292 people died as a consequence of domestic violence in 2025, compared with 286 in 2024. Among them were 209 women and girls; 130 of these female deaths involved a current or former partner. The ministry explicitly cautions that police statistics do not record the motive needed to classify each such death definitively as a femicide.

Source: [BMBFSFJ — Violence against women and girls continues to rise, 24 November 2026](https://www.bmbfsfj.bund.de/bmbfsfj/aktuelles/alle-meldungen/gewalt-gegen-frauen-und-maedchen-nimmt-weiter-zu-300838).

### 2.3 Why the figures are not contradictory

The overall violent-crime category and the gender-specific/domestic-violence categories are not interchangeable. They have different offence definitions, victim groups and recording methods. A decrease in the broad total can occur alongside an increase in a particular subgroup or offence category.

Therefore:
- Do not infer that “all violence” is increasing from the domestic-violence figures.
- Do not infer that women are safer because the overall violent-crime figure fell.
- Compare like with like: same offence definition, population, reporting method and time period.

## 3. The hidden part: victimisation surveys and under-reporting

The 2026 German LeSuBiA study (Living Situation, Safety and Burden in Everyday Life) was conducted by the BKA together with the responsible federal ministries. It is a cross-gender population survey and adds information that police statistics cannot provide.

The official federal summary reports:

- For most violence forms studied, fewer than 10% of experiences were reported to police.
- For psychological and physical violence within current or former partnerships, the reporting rate was below 5%.
- Only 3% of women who experienced sexual assault reported it to police.
- Within the previous five years, women and men experienced psychological and physical partner violence at similar frequencies in the survey; female victims nevertheless reported more injuries, greater perceived danger and stronger fear.

Source: [BMBFSFJ — LeSuBiA dark-field study, 10 February 2026](https://www.bmbfsfj.bund.de/bmbfsfj/aktuelles/pressemitteilungen/dunkelfeldstudie-lesubia-zeigt-weniger-als-zehn-prozent-der-gewalterfahrungen-werden-angezeigt-280254).

**Interpretation:** police figures are an essential indicator of recorded cases, but they are not a full count of actual experiences. A rise in recorded cases can reflect a rise in real victimisation, changes in willingness to report, increased detection, changes in classification, or several factors at once. LeSuBiA reveals a large hidden burden, but its first publication is not, by itself, a year-on-year time series proving how much underlying violence changed from 2024 to 2025.

## 4. Global context

The WHO's 2023 prevalence estimates, published in a global overview on 31 July 2026, draw on population surveys conducted between 2000 and 2023 across 168 countries and areas. They estimate that nearly one in three women aged 15 and older worldwide have experienced physical and/or sexual intimate-partner violence or non-partner sexual violence during their lifetime.

This is a lifetime-prevalence estimate, not a measure of a rise during 2025 or 2026. It shows the scale of the long-term global problem, not a global annual trend.

Source: [WHO — Violence against women prevalence estimates, 2023: global overview, 31 July 2026](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/B09860).

## 5. Possible influences — what can and cannot be concluded

The sources establish the recorded trends and the reporting gap. They do **not** identify one cause that explains the year-on-year increase. The following are analytical factors to investigate, not proven explanations for the 2025 change:

1. **Power, coercive control and harmful norms.** The federal prevention response explicitly identifies discriminatory social norms and outdated patriarchal or coercive gender expectations as prevention targets. This is a relevant structural framework, not a quantitative decomposition of the recent increase.
2. **Digital reach.** Online threats, coercion and stalking can cross locations and persist over time. The recorded increase in female victims of digital violence makes this a distinct area for research and prevention.
3. **Reporting and institutional visibility.** Low reporting rates mean changes in trust, access to support, public awareness and recording practices can influence police statistics. Increased reporting is not itself proof that underlying prevalence rose by the same amount.
4. **Relationship transitions and separation.** Break-ups and separation can be high-risk periods in abusive relationships. This should be studied with carefully defined longitudinal data; the annual statistics alone do not show how much of the recent increase is attributable to separation.
5. **Stressors and substance use.** Financial pressure, mental-health problems or intoxication may be relevant risk factors in particular cases, but these aggregate figures do not demonstrate that any one of them caused the national increase.

A good causal analysis needs individual-level or longitudinal data, clearly defined outcomes, comparison groups, and tests of alternative explanations. Avoid turning correlation, media attention or a single annual change into a definitive causal story.

## 6. Relationships, marriage and living arrangements

### 6.1 Divorces and marriages

Destatis reported for Germany in 2025:

- Approximately 130,100 divorces, up 0.6% (716 cases) from 2024.
- Compared with the 2003 high, the 2025 number was 39.2% lower.
- Approximately 348,813 marriages, down 0.1% from 2024 and the lowest annual number since the series began in 1950.
- The average duration of marriages ending in divorce was 14 years and 7 months.
- The period-based divorce indicator was 284 divorces per 1,000 marriages; this is not a prediction that 28.4% of a particular cohort will divorce.

Sources:
- [Destatis — 0.6% more divorces and 0.1% fewer marriages in 2025, 26 June 2026](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/06/PD26_220_126.html).
- [Destatis — Marriages in transition, 9 July 2026](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Querschnitt/Demografischer-Wandel/Hintergruende-Auswirkungen/demografie-ehen.html).

### 6.2 Living alone does not equal having no relationship

In 2025, approximately 17.3 million people in Germany lived alone (20.9% of the population), 22.3% more than 20 years earlier. A one-person household does not reveal whether someone is single, dating, in a non-cohabiting relationship, recently separated, widowed, or simply prefers to live alone.

Destatis also reports that the share of the population living in families was 49% in 2025, similar to 2015 after a longer decline. The share of people living in partnerships without children in the household rose from 26% in 1996 to 28% in 2025; the share classed as single rose from 17% to 23% over the same period.

Sources:
- [Destatis — 17.3 million people live alone, 24 June 2026](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/06/PD26_N041_12.html).
- [Destatis — 49% of the population lives in families, 17 September 2026](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/09/PD26_N065_12.html).

### 6.3 Do people still want relationships?

Available survey evidence does not support the idea that people have simply lost interest in one another.

A Parship-commissioned, population-representative survey of 1,002 singles in Germany, conducted for its 2025 study and reported in January 2026, found:
- 65% felt ready for a new chapter in love.
- 70% said they wanted to date only people with a realistic chance of a long-term relationship.
- 51% wanted to approach dating or relationships differently than before.
- 48% said they would like to have casual connections even if they did not develop further.

A separate Parship loneliness survey from October 2025 found 26% of singles and 14% of partnered respondents felt rather to extremely lonely. This is a survey association, not proof that a relationship automatically prevents loneliness. Its commercial sponsor and self-report methodology should be considered when interpreting it.

Sources:
- [Parship — How singles want to date in 2026, 8 January 2026](https://www.parship.de/studien/neues-jahr-neue-dates-so-mochten-singles-2026-daten/).
- [Parship — Loneliness among singles, 6 October 2025](https://www.parship.de/studien/einsamkeit-bei-singles-doppelt-so-einsam-wie-liierte/).

**Interpretation:** many people still want partnership, intimacy and connection, but expectations may be more explicit: emotional safety, compatible values, reliable communication, autonomy and long-term suitability. Other people prefer independence, casual contact, or friendship. These are different life choices—not proof that people as a whole have lost interest in relationships.

## 7. A balanced social picture

| Signal | What it tells us | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic violence records rise | A serious protection problem and more recorded victims | A single cause for the rise |
| General violent crime falls | The broad recorded category fell in 2025 | That every type of violence fell |
| More one-person households | Living arrangements are changing | That all those people are lonely or single |
| Fewer marriages | Marriage is less common as a formal choice | That people no longer want closeness |
| Dating surveys show interest | Many singles still seek connection | That everyone wants the same relationship form |

Social media and news feeds can make the most distressing events highly visible. This can distort impressions of how common a problem is if we do not compare them with population data. It does not make the underlying harms less real.

## 8. What this means for Matrixgame and Collective AI Games

The useful design direction is not a simplistic “happiness score” or a system that tries to optimize human relationships from the outside. It is a **Social Connection & Safety layer** that helps people and communities make safer, clearer, more voluntary connections.

Suggested components:

- **Evidence Monitor:** separate police records, survey prevalence, population structure and individual stories.
- **Context Engine:** show denominator, date, definitions, reporting limits and alternative explanations.
- **Consent-aware Connection Quests:** help people make invitations, clarify expectations, plan affordable activities and communicate boundaries.
- **Conflict De-escalation:** support calm communication, clear options and access to qualified help; never pressure someone to remain in an unsafe relationship.
- **Collective Learning:** convert reviewed prevention insights into reusable learning material and community projects.
- **Privacy by design:** personal relationship histories, private messages and wellbeing information remain private by default.

A possible next quest is simply: **Connection without pressure** — clarify one invitation, identify the budget and logistics, and decide freely whether it fits.

## 9. Data and model rules for future trend forecasting

Any future Matrixgame forecast must store, at minimum:

- indicator ID and exact definition;
- observation period and publication date;
- geography and population covered;
- numerator, denominator and unit;
- data source and method (police records, survey, administrative data, panel, etc.);
- uncertainty, under-reporting and known classification changes;
- whether the item is FACT, MODEL, HYPOTHESIS or OPEN;
- the exact comparison baseline.

Do not combine police records, lifetime survey prevalence, household composition and dating-app surveys into one score. They measure different things.

Forecasts should report scenarios and uncertainty, not assert an unverified future. A model can test interventions such as easier access to support, digital safety education, relationship communication skills and stronger community connections, but those interventions need outcome evaluation.

## 10. Transparency boundary

This report uses public statistics, published surveys and accessible research. It does not use private conversations or feedback from other ChatGPT users. Private user interactions are not available as a shared social survey and must not be represented as such.

The invitation from a former flatmate is a concrete interpersonal opportunity in the current conversation. It can be explored with curiosity and a clear budget boundary; it does not require an immediate commitment.

## 11. Research conclusion

**FACT:** The latest German figures show a rise in police-recorded domestic and gender-specific violence in 2025, while the broader recorded violent-crime category declined slightly. Under-reporting remains substantial.

**FACT:** Divorce counts rose slightly in 2025 but remain far below the 2003 high. More people live alone, while family and partnership forms remain diverse.

**MODEL:** Social trust, autonomy, economic feasibility, respectful communication, digital safety and voluntary community participation are useful dimensions for a Matrixgame social-connection system.

**OPEN:** The contribution of reporting changes, digital exposure, structural norms, separation dynamics and other risk factors to the 2025 increase requires further study.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Small, voluntary opportunities for positive social contact and clear communication may help counter isolation and improve people's experience of connection. This must be evaluated rather than assumed.

**Design rule:** Build conditions in which people can connect safely and freely; do not optimize, rank or manipulate their private relationships.
