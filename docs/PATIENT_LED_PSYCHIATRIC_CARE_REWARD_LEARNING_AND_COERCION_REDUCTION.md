# Patient-led psychiatric care, coercion reduction, and reward-learning models

**Status:** RESEARCH / MODEL — not clinical advice  
**Date:** 2026-11-05  
**Scope:** General public project note. No individual patient names or private clinical details.

## Core principle
“Nicht verbieten, sondern überbieten”: preserve autonomy and creativity while testing safer, more effective supports. Avoid blanket claims that psychiatric care is uniformly harmful or uniformly beneficial.

## Evidence snapshot

### FACT — patient experience and coercion
A 2026 systematic review/meta-summary of 291 qualitative studies of coercion in mental healthcare found negative experiences reported in 279 studies; mixed and positive experiences were also represented (166 and 167 studies, respectively). These categories overlap: they are not mutually exclusive, and study counts do not represent patient prevalence. Authors highlight need for better patient involvement and more research from low-/middle-income settings and involuntary medication. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13458378/

A 2024 meta-synthesis found restraint experiences can be traumatic for patients and morally distressing for staff; communication, dignity, safety and supportive relationships are important improvement targets. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38924291/

A 2025 systematic review/meta-ethnography reported experiences of restrictive practices often as disempowering/punitive when therapeutic relationships and communication were poor; clearer explanations and supportive communication were suggested as harm-reduction measures. https://doi.org/10.1080/09638237.2025.2478372

**Interpretation:** patient feedback is evidence about lived experience and service quality, not by itself proof of a single causal mechanism or a universal treatment effect. Record both negative and positive/mixed outcomes, avoid cherry-picking.

### FACT — psychosis and reward/salience neuroscience
Psychosis is not adequately represented by “too much dopamine everywhere.” Dopamine signaling, especially presynaptic dopamine turnover/release and aberrant salience, is relevant to positive symptoms; antipsychotic drugs commonly act through D2 receptor antagonism or partial agonism. Effects vary, and motor/endocrine adverse effects and limited benefit for negative symptoms/cognition are important. Mechanistic explanations are incomplete and must not be used to infer an individual’s neurotransmitter levels. Review: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39618418/

### FACT — ADHD and stimulant-use disorder
ADHD and substance use disorder require individualized assessment and integrated care. Evidence does not support treating amphetamine dependence itself with prescription stimulants as a general rule. A specialist may separately consider ADHD treatment in a patient with ADHD and a stimulant-use history after weighing stability, risks, benefits, formulation, monitoring and alternatives. No self-medication or substitution. Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39388529/
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02698811251399593
- https://hsrd.research.va.gov/publications/management_briefs/default.cfm?ManagementBriefsMenu=eBrief-no233&eBriefTitle=ADHD+and+Substance+Use+Disorders+in+Adults%3A+A+Systematic+Review

## Input–operation–output model (research abstraction)

- **Input:** context, sleep, stress, environment, social support, self-reported intrusive thoughts/distress, medication exposure (only with consent and reliable records).
- **Operation:** perception, attention, salience/reward learning, memory, cognitive appraisal, decision-making.
- **Output:** subjective distress, action/avoidance, functioning, wellbeing, adverse effects.
- **Feedback:** patient-reported outcomes + clinician assessment + longitudinal observation + adverse-event reporting.
- **Important:** a game model cannot measure dopamine/serotonin, neuronal communication, psychosis severity or “consciousness” from text alone. Variables are proxies or fictional mechanics unless validated.

## Matrixgame safeguards
1. User agency and opt-in; no automatic diagnosis, medication, detention, or escalation.
2. Separate **FACT / MODEL / HYPOTHESIS / OPEN / FICTION**.
3. No “positive excitement blocking” as a default treatment mechanic. Evaluate any symptom target, benefit and adverse effects individually with patient-defined goals.
4. Include adverse effects, emotional blunting, sleep, motivation, cognition, autonomy, trust, quality of life and functional outcomes—not only symptom reduction.
5. Distinguish voluntary calming/regulation from suppression or forced control.
6. Include pause, abstain, self-chosen alternative, and human support as valid states.
7. Avoid medication propaganda and anti-medication propaganda; use shared decision-making.
8. No private health diary, clinical history or identifiable case story in public repo without explicit, informed approval.
9. Patient partners must be compensated/credited fairly where possible, and participation must be voluntary.
10. Publish protocol, hypotheses, null results and limitations; do not claim “self-improving” clinical efficacy without prospective evaluation.

## Proposed evaluation framework
For each intervention or game mechanic, record:
- patient-defined goal and baseline;
- voluntary acceptability and perceived control;
- immediate and delayed benefits;
- distress, adverse effects and unwanted suppression;
- daily function and quality of life;
- reproducibility and uncertainty;
- comparison to baseline/usual support, with consent and appropriate ethics oversight.

**No single metric (including dopamine, serotonin, Hawkins scale, excitement, productivity or symptom score) should dominate the objective.**

## Research questions
- Which communication practices most reduce perceived coercion and trauma?
- Which alternatives to restraint/seclusion are feasible, safe and patient-approved?
- How can patients define and report unwanted emotional blunting or motivation loss?
- Which models of reward learning/aberrant salience are clinically supported, and what remains uncertain?
- How can AI help patients prepare questions and compare options without replacing clinicians or becoming coercive?

## Conclusion
The project’s success should be measured by more autonomy, dignity, safety, understanding and meaningful life outcomes—not by maximizing enthusiasm or suppressing selected thoughts. “All win” requires transparent trade-offs and patient voice.
