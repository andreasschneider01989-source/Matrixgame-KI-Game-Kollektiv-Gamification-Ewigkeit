# Human Flourishing and Positive Feedback Framework
**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Status:** MODEL / design framework  
**Scope:** Generic, voluntary and non-clinical. No personal profile or private health data is stored here.

## 1. Purpose

Matrixgame should help participants create conditions for safer, healthier, more meaningful and more cooperative lives. This is a design goal, not a claim that a game can diagnose, treat or reliably predict a person's mental state.

The framework is intended to:
- support agency rather than control;
- encourage manageable action rather than pressure;
- connect motivation with real-world wellbeing;
- make progress visible without tying human worth to productivity;
- preserve uncertainty, consent and privacy.

## 2. Value ordering for system design

Use the following as a decision-priority proposal:

1. **Peace and safety** — avoid preventable harm and destabilising escalation.
2. **Wellbeing** — support physical, emotional and social needs.
3. **Freedom and agency** — preserve informed choice and the ability to stop or change direction.
4. **Truth and transparency** — represent evidence, uncertainty and limitations honestly.
5. **Communication and cooperation** — enable mutual understanding and fair participation.
6. **Learning, creativity and contribution** — create opportunities for discovery, play and useful work.
7. **Regeneration** — include rest, recovery, maintenance and long-term resilience.

The ordering is a project value model. It is not an empirically derived universal law.

## 3. A small positive-feedback loop

Use a lightweight loop rather than a complicated self-optimisation score:

### Observe
Ask what is happening now, in plain language. Examples:
- energy / activation,
- tension / stress,
- interest / curiosity,
- hope / confidence,
- connection / belonging,
- sense of agency,
- need for rest.

A participant may optionally self-rate one or more of these on a 0–10 personal scale. Such numbers are private reflection aids, not validated psychological measurements.

### Choose
Select one small, controllable step:
- 5–20 minutes of a clearly bounded task,
- a short break or walk,
- a meal, water or sleep preparation,
- a message or conversation with a trusted person,
- a small code change or test,
- closing an open loop or reducing a source of overload.

### Act and inspect
Afterward ask:
- What changed?
- Did the action help, do nothing, or add strain?
- What should be repeated, simplified or stopped?

### Preserve
Record only what the participant chooses to keep. A useful note can be just:
`Context → Small action → Observed effect → Next adjustment`.

## 4. Reflective use of the Hawkins Map

David R. Hawkins' Map of Consciousness can be offered as an optional symbolic vocabulary for reflecting on states such as fear, courage, willingness, acceptance, reason, love, joy and peace.

**Scientific boundary:** The numerical levels and calibration claims should not be presented as objective measurements, diagnoses, ranks of human worth or reliable predictions. The map's calibration method is associated with applied kinesiology / manual muscle testing. A 2008 literature review concluded that the available evidence was insufficient to establish diagnostic accuracy or validity for applied-kinesiology muscle testing. A 2014 double-blind randomized study reported 80 correct identifications in 151 trials (53%; not statistically significant versus chance under the reported test). These findings do not establish the Hawkins numbers as a validated psychometric scale.

Sources:
- Hall, Lewith, Brien & Little (2008), “A review of the literature in applied and specialised kinesiology,” PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18334813/
- Hall et al. (2008), full review summary: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK76251/
- 2014 double-blind randomized applied-kinesiology study, PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24607076/
- Publisher's description of the Map and its method (primary description, not independent validation): https://veritaspub.com/map-of-consciousness/

### Safe design rule
Use the map as a metaphor or conversation prompt only. Do not assign an objective Hawkins number to a person, team, project or society. Do not treat a low state as failure or shame. Ask instead:

**“Which next action could support more courage, willingness, acceptance, connection or calm in this situation?”**

If numerical wellbeing tracking is ever added, it should use separately justified instruments for their intended purpose, informed consent, privacy protections and appropriate human oversight. Do not substitute the Hawkins scale for validated instruments or professional assessment.

## 5. The future-simulator connection

A future-scenario module may help users reason about choices and conditions. It should not claim to know a person's future.

A scenario should contain:
- current assumptions and data date,
- multiple plausible paths,
- skills/resources/constraints that can be influenced,
- milestones and options,
- uncertainty ranges or qualitative confidence,
- early indicators that would change the scenario,
- a practical next action.

A scenario should never be framed as destiny. Keep the distinction between:
- **FACT:** documented observation or sourced data;
- **MODEL:** simplified representation with stated assumptions;
- **HYPOTHESIS:** testable expectation;
- **OPEN:** unresolved question;
- **FICTION:** an intentionally imagined game world.

Personal career, wellbeing and health-related context must remain in a private/local store unless an individual explicitly chooses to share specific information. Public repositories should store generic schemas and non-sensitive example data only.

## 6. Ethical gamification

Reward:
- learning and skill evidence,
- reproducible experiments,
- helpful collaboration,
- careful corrections,
- honest uncertainty,
- useful negative results,
- accessible explanations,
- sustainable pacing and recovery.

Avoid:
- random reward schedules that encourage compulsive checking,
- streaks that shame participants for resting,
- public wellbeing rankings,
- manipulating emotions to maximise engagement,
- conflating game XP with real-world worth or mental health.

## 7. Suggested measurements for experiments

For a voluntary, non-clinical reflection module, candidate process measures include:
- chosen goal completed or revised,
- perceived usefulness of the action,
- participant-reported effort,
- whether the action increased or reduced strain,
- return to the task after a break,
- whether the participant felt informed and in control.

Keep each measure purpose-specific. A change in a self-rating is not proof of a clinical effect or a causal mechanism.

## 8. Core statement

**Progress should make life more workable, not make a person feel they must constantly perform.**

A good Matrixgame loop helps someone notice, choose, act, learn, connect and recover. The system should remain a tool for human flourishing—not a judge of the human being.
